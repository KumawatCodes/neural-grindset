## Unit IV – Startup in IT: Key Notes

## 1. Planning a Startup Business in IT Sector

When planning an IT startup, you must prepare a **business plan**. Main sections (as per syllabus):[[collegesidekick](https://www.collegesidekick.com/study-docs/24676050)]

1. **Executive Summary**
    
    - Short overview of the entire plan (1–2 pages).
        
    - Includes: business idea, mission, products/services, target market, funding needs, key goals.
        
    - Written last but appears first.
        
2. **General Company Description**
    
    - Legal form: Pvt. Ltd., LLP, OPC, etc.
        
    - Name, location, founders, vision, mission, objectives.
        
    - Industry: IT sector (software, services, product, SaaS, etc.).
        
3. **Products and Services**
    
    - What you are offering: e.g., mobile app, SaaS product, cloud services, cybersecurity solutions, AI tools, IT consulting.
        
    - Unique selling points (USPs) and advantages over competitors.
        
4. **Marketing Plan**
    
    - Target customers (students, SMEs, enterprises, government, etc.).
        
    - Market size and growth.
        
    - Pricing strategy (subscription, one‑time, freemium).
        
    - Promotion: social media, SEO, content marketing, partnerships, events.
        
    - Sales strategy: direct sales, partners, online platform.
        
5. **Operational Plan**
    
    - How the business will run day‑to‑day:
        
        - Office location, infrastructure.
            
        - Technology stack (cloud, servers, tools).
            
        - Development process (Agile, DevOps).
            
        - Staffing requirements.
            
6. **Management and Organization**
    
    - Organizational structure: CEO, CTO, developers, designers, QA, marketing, sales, support.
        
    - Roles and responsibilities of founders and key team members.
        
    - Advisors, mentors, board (if any).
        
7. **Personal Financial Statement** (for founders)
    
    - Personal assets, liabilities, net worth.
        
    - Shows how much personal capital the founder can invest.
        
8. **Startup Expenses and Capitalization**
    
    - Initial costs: hardware, software licenses, office setup, salaries, marketing, legal, IP registration.
        
    - Capitalization: how much equity, debt, angel/VC funding, grants.
        
9. **Financial Plan**
    
    - Revenue model and projections (1–5 years).
        
    - Break‑even analysis.
        
    - Cash flow, profit & loss, balance sheet projections.
        
    - Funding requirements and use of funds.
        
10. **Appendices**
    
    - Supporting documents: resumes of founders, detailed market research, technical designs, patents/trademarks, contracts, letters of interest.
        
11. **Refining the Plan**
    
    - Review and revise based on feedback from mentors, investors, incubators.
        
    - Update with real data, pilot results, customer feedback.

## 2. Important IP Time Limits (Often Asked in Exams)

You specifically asked for questions like:

- “Patent valid for how many years?”
    
- “How many times can it be renewed?”
    
- “What is the duration of copyright?” etc.
    

Here are the key points (India context):[[en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_of_patent)]

## Patent

- **Term**: **20 years** from the **date of filing** the patent application.[[epo](https://www.epo.org/en/service-support/faq/patents-and-ip/where-patent-valid-and-how-long-does-it-last)]
    
- **Started from**: filing date (not grant date).
    
- **Renewal**: Cannot be extended beyond 20 years. Must pay **annual maintenance fees** to keep it in force; if not paid, it lapses.[[babariaip](https://babariaip.com/blog/when-and-how-to-renew-your-patents-trademarks-and-copyrights/)]
    
- **Extensions**: In India, generally **no extension** beyond 20 years. (Some countries allow limited extensions for regulatory delays, but not in typical Indian exam context.)
    

**Exam‑style facts**:

- “A patent in India is valid for _ years from the filing date.” → **20 years**.
    
- “Can a patent be renewed after 20 years?” → **No**.
    
- “What happens if annual fees are not paid?” → **Patent lapses**.
    

## Copyright

- Protects literary, artistic, musical, dramatic works, films, software code, etc.
    
- **Duration in India**:
    
    - For most works: **Life of author + 60 years** after the end of the year in which the author dies.
        
    - For anonymous/pseudonymous works, cinematograph films, sound recordings, government works: **60 years** from the end of the year of publication.[[babariaip](https://babariaip.com/blog/when-and-how-to-renew-your-patents-trademarks-and-copyrights/)]
        
- **Renewal**: Generally **no renewal** needed; protection is automatic and lasts for the full term.[[babariaip](https://babariaip.com/blog/when-and-how-to-renew-your-patents-trademarks-and-copyrights/)]
    

**Exam‑style facts**:

- “Copyright in India lasts for _ years after the author’s death.” → **60 years**.
    
- “Does copyright need to be renewed every few years?” → **No**.
    

## Trademark

- **Initial registration period**: **10 years** from the date of registration.
    
- **Renewal**: Can be **renewed indefinitely** for successive **10‑year** periods by paying renewal fees.[[babariaip](https://babariaip.com/blog/when-and-how-to-renew-your-patents-trademarks-and-copyrights/)]
    
- If not renewed within the grace period, it may be removed from the register.
    

**Exam‑style facts**:

- “A trademark is initially registered for _ years.” → **10 years**.
    
- “How many times can a trademark be renewed?” → **Indefinitely** (unlimited renewals for 10 years each time).
    
- “What is the renewal period for trademark?” → **10 years** each time.
    

## Industrial Design (if mentioned)

- **Term**: Initially **10 years**, extendable by **5 years** (total **15 years**).
    
- Not renewable beyond 15 years.
    

For exam MCQs, focus on:

|IP Type|Term in India|Renewable?|From which date?|
|---|---|---|---|
|Patent|20 years|No (cannot extend beyond 20)|Filing date of application|
|Copyright|Life + 60 years (or 60 yrs)|No|Death/publication year|
|Trademark|10 years (initial)|Yes, indefinitely (10 yrs)|Registration date|

1. The main purpose of a business plan for an IT startup is to:  
    – Outline the business idea, strategy, market, operations, and financial projections to guide the startup and attract investors.

    – A short overview of the entire plan, written last but placed first.

    – General Company Description.

    – Products and Services.

    – Day‑to‑day running: office, technology stack, development process, and staffing.

    – Management and Organization.

    – Personal Financial Statement.
    
9. Initial costs like hardware, software licenses, office setup, and salaries are listed in:  
    – Startup Expenses and Capitalization.
    
10. Revenue projections, break‑even analysis, and cash flow for 3–5 years are part of:  
    – Financial Plan.
    
11. Supporting documents like resumes, detailed market research, and patent certificates are placed in:  
    – Appendices.
    
12. Refining the business plan mainly means:  
    – Reviewing and updating it based on feedback, pilot results, and real data.
    
13. A startup develops a new SaaS product for project management. Which section will describe its subscription model and pricing?  
    – Marketing Plan and Financial Plan (pricing in marketing; revenue in financial).
    
14. A startup founder wants to show how much personal money they can invest. Which document helps?  
    – Personal Financial Statement.
    
15. In India, the term of a **patent** is:  
    – **20 years** from the filing date of the patent application.
    
16. From which date does the 20‑year patent term start in India?  
    – From the **filing date** of the application.
    
17. Can a patent in India be extended beyond 20 years?  
    – **No**, it cannot be extended beyond 20 years.
    
18. What happens if annual maintenance fees for a patent are not paid?  
    – The patent **lapses** before the 20‑year term.
    
19. In India, copyright for most literary/artistic works lasts for:  
    – **Life of the author + 60 years** after the end of the year of death.
    
20. For a cinematograph film in India, copyright duration is:  
    – **60 years** from the end of the year of publication.
    
21. Does copyright in India need to be renewed every few years?  
    – **No**, it is automatic and lasts for the full term without renewal.
    
22. A trademark is initially registered in India for:  
    – **10 years** from the date of registration.
    
23. How many times can a trademark be renewed in India?  
    – **Indefinitely** (unlimited renewals).
    
24. Each renewal of a trademark in India is for:  
    – **10 years**.

    – Renew the trademark for another 10 years, and continue doing so indefinitely.

    – **10 March 2040** (20 years from filing date).

28. The same startup wants to protect its unique app name and logo. Which IP is most relevant?  
    – **Trademark**.
    
29. A startup invents a new algorithm and technical method to compress data. Which IP is most suitable?  
    – **Patent** (if it meets novelty, inventive step, and industrial applicability).

    – **Zoho** or **Freshworks** (both sell their own software products globally).
