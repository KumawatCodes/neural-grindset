## 1. Law and Ethics in Information Security

**Law vs Ethics**:[[slideshare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/law-and-ethics-in-information-securitypptx/263330840)]

- **Law**:
    
    - Rules made by government.
        
    - **Mandatory** – must follow.
        
    - Has **sanctions/punishments** (fine, imprisonment).
        
- **Ethics**:
    
    - Moral principles guiding behavior.
        
    - Not always legally mandatory.
        
    - No direct legal punishment, but can lead to loss of trust, reputation, professional consequences.
        

**In information security**:

- **Law** tells you what you **must** do (e.g., protect data under IT Act, report cyber incidents).
    
- **Ethics** tells you what you **should** do (e.g., be honest about vulnerabilities, not misuse access, respect privacy).
    

**Key idea**:

- Ethical behavior supports compliance with laws.
    
- Organizations must model strong ethical behavior to reduce legal liability.[[scribd](https://www.scribd.com/presentation/612541299/Unit-5)]

## 2. Organizational Liability and the Need for Counsel

**Organizational Liability**:

- If an organization fails to encourage or model strong ethical behavior, it can be **liable** for:
    
    - Data breaches due to weak security.
        
    - Privacy violations.
        
    - Harm to customers, employees, or partners.
        
- Liability includes:
    
    - **Criminal liability** (if laws broken).
        
    - **Civil liability** (compensation for damages).[[slideshare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/law-and-ethics-in-information-securitypptx/263330840)]
        

**Due Care and Due Diligence**:

- **Due care**: Taking reasonable steps to protect information and systems.
    
- **Due diligence**: Regularly checking, monitoring, and improving security practices.
    
- Organizations must show both to reduce liability.[[slideshare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/law-and-ethics-in-information-securitypptx/263330840)]
    

**Need for Counsel**:

- Organizations need **legal counsel** (lawyers) to:
    
    - Understand cyber and data protection laws.
        
    - Draft policies and contracts.
        
    - Handle disputes, investigations, and prosecutions.
        
    - Advise on compliance with IT Act and other laws.[[scribd](https://www.scribd.com/presentation/612541299/Unit-5)]

## 3. Policy Versus Law

**Policy**:

- Internal rules set by an organization (e.g., company IT policy, email policy, password policy).
    
- Apply only to employees/members of that organization.
    
- Not enforceable by government, but can be enforced by organization (e.g., disciplinary action).
    

**Law**:

- Rules made by government, enforceable by state.
    
- Apply to everyone in the jurisdiction.
    
- Violation can lead to criminal/civil punishment.
    

**Key differences**:

|Aspect|Policy|Law|
|---|---|---|
|Maker|Organization|Government|
|Scope|Employees/members only|All people in jurisdiction|
|Enforcement|Organization (disciplinary action)|State (court, police, penalty)|
|Punishment|Job loss, warning, etc.|Fine, imprisonment, compensation|

In security:

- Policies implement laws inside the organization.
    
- Example: IT Act says “protect data”; company policy says “use strong passwords, no sharing”.[[scribd](https://www.scribd.com/presentation/612541299/Unit-5)]

## 4. Cyber Crime

**Cyber Crime**: Any criminal activity using computers, networks, or digital devices.[[linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aarif-hussain-khan_current-cyber-crime-landscape-in-india-cyber-activity-7337452409683640321-SvWY)]

**Common types**:[[theprint](https://theprint.in/india/cybercrime-saw-24-spike-in-2025-indians-lost-rs-22495-crore-mainly-in-investment-scams/2859930/)]

- **Phishing**: Fake emails/messages to steal credentials, money.
    
    - About **80%** of reported cyber crimes are phishing.[[linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aarif-hussain-khan_current-cyber-crime-landscape-in-india-cyber-activity-7337452409683640321-SvWY)]
        
- **Ransomware**: Malware that locks data and demands payment.
    
    - Global ransomware damages expected to reach **$57 billion by 2025**.[[linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aarif-hussain-khan_current-cyber-crime-landscape-in-india-cyber-activity-7337452409683640321-SvWY)]
        
- **Identity Theft**: Stealing personal data to impersonate someone.
    
- **Online Fraud**: Fake financial apps, fraudulent websites, UPI scams.
    
- **Investment Scams**: Ponzi schemes, fake stock/crypto trading platforms.
    
- **Sextortion**: Threatening to share private images to extract money.
    
- **Digital Arrest Scams**: Fake “police/legal” calls forcing victims to pay.

## 6. Need for Cyber Law in India

**Why needed**:[[mcolegals](https://www.mcolegals.in/kb-details?title=cyber-law%3A-issue-3---critical-analysis-of-the-information-technology-act%2C-2000&kid=296)]

- Rapid digitization → more online transactions, data, services.
    
- Increase in cyber crimes (fraud, phishing, ransomware, identity theft).
    
- Need to:
    
    - Define cyber crimes and punishments.
        
    - Protect data privacy.
        
    - Regulate electronic contracts, digital signatures.
        
    - Provide legal framework for cyber investigations and evidence.
        

**Main cyber law in India**:[[mcolegals](https://www.mcolegals.in/kb-details?title=cyber-law%3A-issue-3---critical-analysis-of-the-information-technology-act%2C-2000&kid=296)]

- **Information Technology Act, 2000 (IT Act, 2000)**
    
    - Only one legislation to regulate all spheres of cyberspace in India.[[mcolegals](https://www.mcolegals.in/kb-details?title=cyber-law%3A-issue-3---critical-analysis-of-the-information-technology-act%2C-2000&kid=296)]
        
    - Covers:
        
        - Electronic contracts
            
        - Digital signatures
            
        - Cyber crimes
            
        - Data protection (some provisions)
            
        - Jurisdiction in cyber cases
            
- Amended in **2008** (IT Act Amendment) to add more cyber crime provisions and data protection.
    

**Other relevant laws**:

- Indian Penal Code (for general crimes).
    
- Data protection laws (e.g., Digital Personal Data Protection Act – recent).

## 7. Ethical Dilemmas in Project Management

**Ethical dilemma**: Situation where you must choose between conflicting moral options.[[pmi](https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/ethical-dilemmas-project-management-7084)]

**Common ethical dilemmas in IT project management**:[[ijettjournal](https://ijettjournal.org/archive/ijett-v67i10p206)]

- **Conflict of interest**:
    
    - Project manager has personal relationship with vendor.
        
- **Pressure to meet deadlines**:
    
    - Cutting testing, hiding bugs, or compromising quality.
        
- **Budget manipulation**:
    
    - “Wired” bids, kickbacks, buy‑in tactics.
        
- **Misreporting progress**:
    
    - Overstating completion to please management/client.
        
- **Unauthorized access**:
    
    - Using system access beyond what is allowed.
        
- **Data misuse**:
    
    - Accessing customer/employee data for personal use.
        
- **Hiding security issues**:
    
    - Not reporting vulnerabilities to avoid delays.
        

**Professional responsibilities**:

- Be honest, transparent, and fair.
    
- Protect integrity of systems and data.
    
- Follow codes of ethics (e.g., PMI, ACM, IEEE).
    
- Avoid conflicts of interest and bribery.

## 8. Principles of Arbitration and Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)

**Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)**:

- Mechanisms to resolve disputes **outside traditional courts**.
    
- Faster, cheaper, more flexible, and confidential.[[byjus](https://byjus.com/free-ias-prep/alternative-dispute-resolution/)]
    

**Types of ADR**:[[vajiramandravi](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/alternative-dispute-resolution/)]

1. **Arbitration**
    
    - Parties agree to submit dispute to a neutral **arbitrator** (or tribunal).
        
    - Arbitrator gives **final and binding** decision (arbitral award).
        
    - Private, but judicial in nature.
        
2. **Mediation**
    
    - Neutral **mediator** helps parties negotiate.
        
    - Mediator does **not** impose decision.
        
    - Outcome is **voluntary** and **non‑binding** unless agreed.
        
3. **Conciliation**
    
    - Similar to mediation but conciliator is more **proactive**.
        
    - May suggest solutions.
        
    - Usually non‑binding until settlement reached.
        
4. **Judicial Settlements / Lok Adalats**
    
    - Court‑managed settlements, village courts.
        
5. **Negotiation**
    
    - Direct discussion between parties to settle.
        

**Principles of Arbitration**:[[sist.sathyabama.ac](https://sist.sathyabama.ac.in/sist_coursematerial/uploads/SAL1002.pdf)]

- Parties choose arbitrator by consensus.
    
- Neutral, unbiased tribunal.
    
- Fair hearing for both sides.
    
- Decision is **final and binding**.
    
- Aim: **speedy** and **cost‑effective** resolution.
    

**Under IT Act**:

- Two‑tier dispute resolution:
    
    1. Adjudication of disputes.
        
    2. Appeal against adjudication outcome.[[ikigailaw](https://www.ikigailaw.com/article/261/dispute-resolution-framework-under-the-information-technology-act-2000)]

## 9. Hands‑on Tools for Cyber Security

Cybersecurity tools protect systems, networks, and data.[[intruder](https://www.intruder.io/blog/cybersecurity-tools)]

**Common types**:

1. **Antivirus / Endpoint Protection**
    
    - Detects and removes malware.
        
    - Examples: ESET, Bitdefender, Sophos, Avast.[[intruder](https://www.intruder.io/blog/cybersecurity-tools)]
        
2. **Firewall**
    
    - Controls network traffic (in/out).
        
    - Blocks unauthorized access.
        
    - Example: Cisco Secure Firewall.[[sentinelone](https://www.sentinelone.com/cybersecurity-101/cybersecurity/cyber-security-tools/)]
        
3. **SIEM (Security Information and Event Management)**
    
    - Collects and analyzes logs from systems, networks.
        
    - Detects threats in real time.
        
    - Example: **Splunk Enterprise Security**, Graylog.[[imperial.ac](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/admin-services/ict/about-ict/meet-the-team/tooling/antivirus,-it,-ict/)]
        
4. **Vulnerability Scanner**
    
    - Finds weaknesses: missing patches, misconfigurations, bugs.
        
    - Example: **Intruder**, **Qualys**, Nessus.[[intruder](https://www.intruder.io/blog/cybersecurity-tools)]
        
5. **Intrusion Detection/Prevention Systems (IDS/IPS)**
    
    - Detect/block suspicious network activity.
        
6. **Encryption Tools**
    
    - Encrypt data for confidentiality.
        
7. **Penetration Testing Tools**
    
    - Simulate attacks to test security.
        
    - Example: Metasploit, Burp Suite.
        

For exam: know names and basic purpose (antivirus = malware, firewall = traffic control, SIEM = log analysis, vulnerability scanner = find weaknesses).

## 10. Case Studies on Cyber Crimes (General Patterns)

You may get scenario questions like:

- Company data encrypted by ransomware → attacker demands payment.
    
- Employee receives fake email → clicks link → credentials stolen → bank fraud.
    
- Customer data leaked → company sued for privacy violation.
    
- Fake app → investment scam → huge financial loss.
    

Key points:

- Identify type of cyber crime.
    
- Identify weakness (no training, weak security, no policy).
    
- Identify legal/ethical issue (privacy violation, negligence, conflict of interest).
