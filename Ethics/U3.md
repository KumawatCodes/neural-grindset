
## Unit III – Government Funding and Startup Schemes: Key Notes

## 1. What are Startups?

- A startup = a newly established company, usually small, aiming to develop a scalable business model, often with technology/innovation focus.
    
- Often characterized by:
    
    - High risk, high uncertainty
        
    - Innovation in product/service/process
        
    - Potential for fast growth
        
    - Limited initial resources
        

In India, the government has a specific definition for “Startup” (for policy benefits):

- Generally, a company/LLP/partnership:
    
    - Established not more than 10 years from date of incorporation,
        
    - Annual turnover not more than ₹100 crore,
        
    - Working towards innovation, development, or improvement of products/services.
        

---

## 2. Start-up India: Vision and Benefits

**Start-up India** is a flagship government initiative (launched in 2016) to build a strong ecosystem for innovation and entrepreneurship.

**Main objectives**: [general GK]

- Promote innovation and entrepreneurship across India.
    
- Support startups with funding, mentorship, and regulatory ease.
    
- Create jobs and boost economic growth.
    

**Key benefits for startups under Start-up India**:

- **Funding support**:
    
    - ₹10,000 crore Fund of Funds for Startups (FFS) managed by SIDBI, to invest in startup‑focused venture funds.
        
    - Credit guarantee schemes for banks to lend to startups.
        
- **Tax benefits**:
    
    - 3‑year tax exemption for eligible startups (subject to conditions and certification).
        
    - Exemption from capital gains tax for certain investments (via specific schemes).
        
- **Easy compliance**:
    
    - Simplized public procurement (exemptions from prior experience/turnover criteria).
        
    - Faster patent examination and lower patent filing fees.
        
    - Self‑certification under many labour/e‑laws.
        
- **Incubation and mentorship**:
    
    - Support from government‑recognized incubators.
        
    - Mentorship programs, networking events, pitch competitions.
        
- **IPR support**:
    
    - Rebate of 80% on patent filing fees for startups.
        
    - Fast‑tracked examination of patent applications.
        

---

## 3. Startup India Resources

- **Startup India Hub**:
    
    - Single‑point platform for startups to access information, mentorship, funding, and government services.
        
    - Website: startupindia.gov.in
        
- **Recognized incubators**:
    
    - Government‑recognized incubation centers (in universities, IITs, NITs, etc.) providing infrastructure and support.
        
- **Mentorship networks**:
    
    - Industry experts, successful entrepreneurs, and professionals offering guidance.
        
- **Funding channels**:
    
    - Venture capital funds, angel investors, private equity, bank loans, and government funds.
        

---

## 4. Bank Loan for Startup Business

- Startups can get loans from banks under:
    
    - **Mudra Yojana** (for women and micro‑entrepreneurs)
        
    - **PMEGY / CGTMSE** (credit guarantee for startups without collateral)
        
    - Special startup loan schemes by public and private banks.
        

**Key points for exams**:

- Many government schemes allow **collateral‑free loans** up to a certain limit for startups.
    
- Startups often need:
    
    - Business plan/proposal
        
    - Financial projections
        
    - Proof of registration (company/LLP)
        
    - KYC of promoters
        
    - Sometimes, DPIIT recognition (Startup India recognition).
        

---

## 5. Start-up India (Programme) – Brief GK

- Launched: **February 2016**.
    
- Prime initiative under: **Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT)**, Ministry of Commerce & Industry.
    
- Goals: foster innovation, create jobs, build startup ecosystem.
    
- Platforms: Startup India Hub, Fund of Funds, incubators, mentorship programs.
    

---

## 6. 10,000 Startups – A NASSCOM Initiative

- **NASSCOM** = National Association of Software and Service Companies, the apex body for India’s IT and product/services industry.
    
- **10,000 Startups** initiative:
    
    - Aim: Support and scale **10,000 tech startups** over several years.
        
    - Focus areas: IT, software, digital products, AI, cloud, cybersecurity, etc.
        
    - Provides:
        
        - Funding support (via partners, VC, angel networks)
            
        - Mentorship from industry experts
            
        - Access to incubators and infrastructure
            
        - Business development and market access
            

For exam:

- Remember: “10,000 Startups – A NASSCOM Initiative” → tech startups in IT sector.
    
- NASSCOM = key industry body supporting IT startups.

## 7. Export Promotion Schemes for IT/Software

## (a) Software Technology Parks (STPs)

- **STP Scheme**:
    
    - Provides infrastructure for software development and export.  
        -设立的 in multiple cities across India.
        
    - Benefits:
        
        - High‑speed internet, power, office space
            
        - Simplified customs/export procedures
            
        - Support for software exports
            
- **STPI** = Software Technology Parks of India (under Ministry of Electronics & IT).
    
- Startups in software can set up units in STPs to export software/services.
    

## (b) Special Economic Zones (SEZ) Scheme

- **SEZ**: Notified area with special economic regulations to promote exports and investment.
    
- Benefits for IT/software companies:
    
    - Duty‑free import of capital goods, raw materials.
        
    - Exemption from certain taxes.
        
    - Simplified compliance.
        
    - Infrastructure support.
        

For startups aiming to export software/services, SEZ/STP can be important locations.

---

## 8. Laws for Startups in India

Key legal and regulatory aspects:

- **Company formation**:
    
    - Options: Private Limited Company, LLP, Partnership, One Person Company (OPC).
        
- **DPIIT recognition**:
    
    - Startups get official recognition by applying on Startup India portal.
        
- **IPR laws**:
    
    - Patents Act, Trademarks Act, Copyright Act, Designs Act.
        
- **Labour and compliance**:
    
    - Self‑certification under many labour laws for startups.
        
- **Tax laws**:
    
    - Income Tax Act (including startup tax exemptions).
        
- **Cyber and data laws**:
    
    - IT Act, 2000, and related rules for data protection, cybercrime.
        

---

## 9. SBIR, STTR, NSF Grants (US‑based but mentioned in syllabus)

These are **US government research/innovation grants**, often studied for comparison/global reference:

- **SBIR** = Small Business Innovation Research Program:
    
    - US federal program to fund small businesses for R&D with potential for commercialization.
        
- **STTR** = Small Technology Transfer Research Program:
    
    - Supports collaboration between small businesses and research institutions for technology development.
        
- **NSF Grants** = National Science Foundation Grants:
    
    - US grants for scientific research, including work that can lead to tech startups.
        

For Indian exam:

- You mainly need to know that these are **US government funding schemes for innovation and small businesses**, not Indian schemes.
    
- Indian equivalent idea: Fund of Funds, CDSC, DST, i3, etc.

## 10. Real‑life Scenario Pattern for Exam

Typical questions:

- “A family wants to start a small software startup. Which funding option is most suitable?”
    
    - Answer direction: Bank loan under startup scheme / Mudra / credit guarantee scheme.
        
- “A tech startup wants to export software. Which institute/scheme helps?”
    
    - Answer direction: STP / SEZ.
        
- “A startup wants fast patent examination and lower fees. Which benefit under Start-up India helps?”
    
    - Answer direction: 80% rebate on patent fees + fast‑track examination.
        
- “When was Start-up India launched?”
    
    - Answer: 2016.
