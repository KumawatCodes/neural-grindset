## Unit I – Ethics: Key Notes (Point-wise)

## 1. Basic ideas: Ethics and Integrity

- Ethics = system of moral principles that guides what is right or wrong in human conduct and decisions.[[bau](https://bau.edu/blog/what-are-business-ethics/)]
    
- Business ethics = applying these moral principles to business decisions, behavior, policies, and relationships (customers, employees, society, government).[[bau](https://bau.edu/blog/what-are-business-ethics/)]
    
- Integrity = honesty plus strong moral principles; doing the right thing consistently, even when nobody is watching.[[boisestate](https://www.boisestate.edu/cobe/blog/2024/02/a-brief-introduction-to-business-ethics/)]
    
- In business, integrity builds trust, reputation, customer loyalty and long‑term success.[[integritysolutions](https://www.integritysolutions.com/blog/seven-principles-business-integrity/)]
    

## 2. Importance of ethics in business world

- Helps companies gain and keep trust of customers, employees, investors and society.[[integritysolutions](https://www.integritysolutions.com/blog/seven-principles-business-integrity/)]
    
- Reduces legal risk: ethical behavior usually aligns with laws and regulations.[[boisestate](https://www.boisestate.edu/cobe/blog/2024/02/a-brief-introduction-to-business-ethics/)]
    
- Improves long‑term profits: ethical companies have stronger brands and more loyal stakeholders.[[boisestate](https://www.boisestate.edu/cobe/blog/2024/02/a-brief-introduction-to-business-ethics/)]
    
- Attracts talent: people prefer to work for organizations with fair and transparent culture.[[integritysolutions](https://www.integritysolutions.com/blog/seven-principles-business-integrity/)]
    

## 3. Improving corporate ethics

Common tools/steps:[[integritysolutions](https://www.integritysolutions.com/blog/seven-principles-business-integrity/)]

- Clear written code of ethics/code of conduct.
    
- Strong tone from the top: leadership must model ethical behavior.
    
- Ethics training and workshops for employees.
    
- Confidential reporting channels (whistle‑blower helpline).
    
- Fair investigation and discipline when code is violated.
    
- Regular audits and reviews of ethical risks (bribery, conflicts of interest, harassment, data misuse, etc.).
    

## 4. Creating an ethical work environment

- Clear policies on conflicts of interest, gifts, harassment, discrimination, privacy, confidentiality.[[boisestate](https://www.boisestate.edu/cobe/blog/2024/02/a-brief-introduction-to-business-ethics/)]
    
- Transparent communication: employees know expectations and can raise concerns safely.[[integritysolutions](https://www.integritysolutions.com/blog/seven-principles-business-integrity/)]
    
- Reward ethical behavior (not just short‑term results).
    
- Equal opportunity and respect for diversity (gender, caste, religion, nationality, etc.).[[hindustantimes](https://www.hindustantimes.com/ht-insight/gender-equality/women-in-manufacturing-engineering-india-s-next-industrial-leap-101772953191591.html)]
    

## 5. Ethical decision making – typical steps

- Identify the problem and stakeholders.
    
- Collect facts (legal rules, company policies, impact on people).
    
- Evaluate options with ethical principles: rights, fairness, consequences, justice.[[boisestate](https://www.boisestate.edu/cobe/blog/2024/02/a-brief-introduction-to-business-ethics/)]
    
- Choose the option that causes least harm and respects rights and fairness.
    
- Implement and then review the outcome.
    

(For MCQs, remember: most frameworks say “recognize problem → collect facts → evaluate options → decide → act → reflect”.)

## 6. Ethics in information technology

- IT ethics = applying moral principles to use, development and management of information systems, software, data and networks.[[purdueglobal](https://www.purdueglobal.edu/blog/information-technology/ethics-information-technology/)]
    
- Very sensitive because IT affects privacy, security, surveillance, AI decisions, social media influence etc.[[purdueglobal](https://www.purdueglobal.edu/blog/information-technology/ethics-information-technology/)]
    

Common ethical issues in IT use:[[geeksforgeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/blogs/ethical-issues-in-information-technology-it/)]

- Privacy: misuse of personal data, tracking users without consent.
    
- Security: weak protection of systems and data, leading to breaches.
    
- Intellectual property: software piracy, illegal downloading, plagiarism.
    
- Digital divide: unequal access to technology and internet.
    
- Cyberbullying, trolling, online harassment.
    
- Misuse of social media (fake news, hate speech, manipulation).
    

## 7. Ethical behavior of IT professionals

- Follow professional codes (like ACM/IEEE codes of ethics).
    
- Protect confidentiality, integrity and availability of information.[[purdueglobal](https://www.purdueglobal.edu/blog/information-technology/ethics-information-technology/)]
    
- Avoid conflicts of interest and bribery.
    
- Be honest about capabilities, deadlines, risks and limitations of systems.
    
- Respect intellectual property and licensing terms.
    

Supporting ethical practices of IT users:

- User awareness and training on safe and responsible IT use.[[purdueglobal](https://www.purdueglobal.edu/blog/information-technology/ethics-information-technology/)]
    
- Clear policies for acceptable use of company systems (internet, email, devices).
    
- Technical controls: access control, monitoring, antivirus, encryption etc.
    
- Reporting channels for security incidents and unethical behavior.
    

## 8. Women empowerment (in tech & engineering angle)

- Women empowerment = expanding women’s ability to make decisions, access resources, education, jobs and leadership.[[hindustantimes](https://www.hindustantimes.com/ht-insight/gender-equality/women-in-manufacturing-engineering-india-s-next-industrial-leap-101772953191591.html)]
    
- In engineering/IT, empowerment means: equal access to STEM education, fair hiring and promotion, safe workplaces, zero tolerance for harassment.[[hindustantimes](https://www.hindustantimes.com/ht-insight/gender-equality/women-in-manufacturing-engineering-india-s-next-industrial-leap-101772953191591.html)]
    
- Benefits to organizations: more creativity, better decision-making, better performance and reputation.[[hindustantimes](https://www.hindustantimes.com/ht-insight/gender-equality/women-in-manufacturing-engineering-india-s-next-industrial-leap-101772953191591.html)]
    

Typical measures:

- Laws against discrimination and harassment.
    
- Maternity benefits and flexible work.
    
- Mentorship and leadership programs for women.[[hindustantimes](https://www.hindustantimes.com/ht-insight/gender-equality/women-in-manufacturing-engineering-india-s-next-industrial-leap-101772953191591.html)]
    

## 9. Ethical aspects of engineering practices

- Engineers must protect public safety, health and environment first.
    
- Must provide honest, accurate information about designs, risks and costs.
    
- Avoid cutting corners on safety or quality to save money.
    
- Respect IP, avoid plagiarism of designs or code.
    
- Report unethical or unsafe practices even if there is pressure from management.

## Unit I – MCQ‑type Practice (30 Questions)

These are for self‑practice (you can convert them to ABCD format while revising). Mix of concept, scenario and light GK/context.

1. “Ethics” in a business context mainly refers to:  
    – the moral principles that guide decisions and behavior in business organizations.
    
2. Which term best describes “doing the right thing even when no one is watching”?  
    – Integrity.
    
3. A company refuses to manipulate its financial statements to impress investors, even though competitors do. This shows:  
    – Integrity and business ethics in financial reporting.
    
4. Which of the following is the strongest reason for ethics in business?  
    – It builds long‑term trust and reputation with stakeholders.
    
5. A manager pressures employees to sell a product by hiding important risk information from customers. Which ethical principle is mainly violated?  
    – Honesty and fairness to customers.
    
6. A company introduces an ethics hotline where employees can report misconduct anonymously. This is mainly a step towards:  
    – Improving corporate ethics and creating an ethical work environment.
    
7. “Tone at the top” in corporate ethics refers to:  
    – The ethical behavior and commitment demonstrated by top management and board.
    
8. A firm has a written code of conduct, regular ethics training, and strong disciplinary actions for violations. These practices aim at:  
    – Institutionalizing ethical behavior in the organization.
    
9. During ethical decision making, which step should logically come first?  
    – Identifying the ethical problem and stakeholders affected.
    
10. An IT company is unsure whether to use user data for a new AI feature. They study legal rules, company values and impact on users before deciding. This represents:  
    – Ethical decision‑making process.
    
11. “Ethics in information technology” mainly deals with:  
    – Responsible use, development and management of information systems and data.
    
12. Downloading licensed software from torrent sites without paying is primarily an issue of:  
    – Intellectual property rights and software piracy.
    
13. A social media company collects detailed user data and sells it to advertisers without clear consent. This primarily raises:  
    – Privacy and informed consent concerns.
    
14. Using weak passwords and ignoring security updates in a company network is unethical because:  
    – It puts customer and employee data at risk of breaches.
    
15. An IT professional knowingly ignores a serious security vulnerability in a banking app to meet a release deadline. Which ethical responsibility is violated most?  
    – Duty to protect confidentiality and security of users’ financial data.
    
16. A student copies full code from an online source and submits as his own project without credit. This is:  
    – Plagiarism and violation of academic and IT ethics.
    
17. Which practice best supports ethical behavior of IT users in an organization?  
    – Providing clear acceptable‑use policies and regular security awareness training.
    
18. Monitoring employee internet usage to prevent data leaks can be ethical if:  
    – The monitoring is transparent, limited, and employees are informed about it.
    
19. In the context of women empowerment in engineering, which action is most aligned with ethics?  
    – Ensuring equal opportunities, equal pay, and a harassment‑free workplace for women engineers.
    
20. A company hires women only for low‑level support roles but keeps leadership roles mainly for men, despite similar qualifications. This situation shows:  
    – Gender discrimination and lack of genuine women empowerment.
    
21. Encouraging girls to pursue STEM education and providing scholarships to them mainly helps to:  
    – Reduce gender gap and promote women empowerment in technical fields.
    
22. An engineering manager ignores a woman engineer’s safety concerns about a design because “she is too junior”. This is unethical because:  
    – It disregards professional responsibility to prioritize safety and respect all professionals.
    
23. An engineer discovers that cheaper material used by the company could fail and harm users. Ethical response is to:  
    – Report the risk to appropriate authority and insist on safe materials, even if costs increase.
    
24. During a project, an engineer is asked to sign off on test reports that were never actually conducted. Ethically, the engineer should:  
    – Refuse to sign and report the request as misconduct.
    
25. Which of the following is a key professional duty of engineers towards society?  
    – Protect public safety, health and environment in all engineering decisions.
    
26. A software developer finds a logic error that will rarely occur but can cause serious financial loss to a few customers. Ethically, the developer should:  
    – Report and fix the error, informing management of potential impact.
    
27. In an ethical work environment, performance appraisal should be based mainly on:  
    – Both results and adherence to ethical behavior and company values.
    
28. Which practice best demonstrates corporate integrity in dealing with suppliers?  
    – Paying on agreed terms, avoiding bribes, and selecting suppliers through fair criteria.
    
29. An IT company introduces flexible work timing and safe transport policies to support its women employees working late shifts. This is mainly an example of:  
    – Women empowerment and creating a safe, inclusive work environment.
    
30. A startup publicly admits a data breach quickly, informs affected customers, and offers support. This response mainly shows:  
    – Ethical responsibility, transparency and respect for stakeholders in an IT context.
