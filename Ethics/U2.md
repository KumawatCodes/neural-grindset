
## Unit II – Intellectual Property (IP): Key Notes

## 1. Concept of Intellectual Property

- Intellectual Property (IP) = creations of the mind like inventions, designs, artistic works, symbols, names, images used in commerce.[[wipo](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/about-ip)]
    
- Law gives creators exclusive rights to use and commercially exploit these creations for a certain time.[[uschamber](https://www.uschamber.com/intellectual-property/intellectual-property-rights-protecting-ideas-for-innovation-and-economic-growth)]
    
- Purpose: encourage innovation and creativity by rewarding creators, and help businesses protect brand and technology.[[wipo](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/about-ip)]
    

Main common types: copyright, trademark, patent, industrial designs, trade secrets, geographical indications, etc.[[blog.ipleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/ipr-description/)]

---

## 2. Copyright

- Protects original literary, artistic and creative works: books, music, films, paintings, software code, etc.[[uspto](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/trademark-patent-copyright)]
    
- Protects expression of idea, not the idea itself.[[osbar](https://www.osbar.org/public/legalinfo/1031_PatentsTrademarksCopyrights.htm)]
    
- Gives owner exclusive rights to reproduce, distribute, perform, display, or make adaptations.[[capitollawpartners](https://capitollawpartners.com/whatisintellectualproperty-copyrights-trademarks-patents)]
    
- Typically automatic on creation (no need to register, though registration helps in legal disputes).[[capitollawpartners](https://capitollawpartners.com/whatisintellectualproperty-copyrights-trademarks-patents)]
    

---

## 3. Trademarks and Types of Marks

- Trademark = word, phrase, symbol, logo, design or combination that identifies and distinguishes the goods of one seller from others.[[openstax](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-intellectual-property/pages/4-4-the-four-types-of-trademarks)]
    
- Service mark = same concept as trademark, but identifies services instead of physical goods.[[openstax](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-intellectual-property/pages/4-4-the-four-types-of-trademarks)]
    

Different kinds of marks (terminology you must remember):[[slideshare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/trademark-and-its-types/74680885)]

- Brand names: word or combination of words used as mark, e.g., unique product name.
    
- Logos / symbols: graphic design, icon or picture standing for brand.
    
- Well‑known marks: marks famous to the public, given extra protection (even if not registered for all classes).
    
- Certification marks: mark owned by a body that certifies quality, origin, material, method, etc. for others (e.g., marks indicating certain quality standard).[[openstax](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-intellectual-property/pages/4-4-the-four-types-of-trademarks)]
    
- Service marks: mark used specifically for services (IT services, consulting, airlines, etc.).[[openstax](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-intellectual-property/pages/4-4-the-four-types-of-trademarks)]
    
- Collective marks: used by members of an association or group to identify goods/services (e.g., professional body designation).[[openstax](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-intellectual-property/pages/4-4-the-four-types-of-trademarks)]

## 4. Patents

- Patent = exclusive right granted for an invention (product or process) that is new, involves inventive step, and is industrially applicable.[[osbar](https://www.osbar.org/public/legalinfo/1031_PatentsTrademarksCopyrights.htm)]
    
- Protects technical inventions: machines, processes, chemical compositions, new methods, etc.[[uspto](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/trademark-patent-copyright)]
    
- Patent owner can stop others from making, using, selling, or importing the invention without permission during patent term.[[uschamber](https://www.uschamber.com/intellectual-property/intellectual-property-rights-protecting-ideas-for-innovation-and-economic-growth)]
    
- After patent expires, invention falls into public domain (anyone can use it).[[uschamber](https://www.uschamber.com/intellectual-property/intellectual-property-rights-protecting-ideas-for-innovation-and-economic-growth)]

## 5. Importance of Patent Information in Business Development

Patent information refers to the technical and legal details published in patent documents.

Why important for business:[[kiteworks](https://www.kiteworks.com/risk-compliance-glossary/intellectual-property/)]

- Helps track latest technological developments and competitor activities.
    
- Avoids duplication of R&D by checking what is already patented.
    
- Helps identify white spaces (areas where no patent exists) → new business opportunities.
    
- Useful for technology licensing, partnerships and strategic planning.

## 6. Identifying social and engineering issues → IP opportunities

In this course, idea is:

- Look at social and engineering problems (e.g., water scarcity, traffic, pollution, digital payment security).
    
- Analyse pain points/needs → think of possible technical or design solutions.
    
- Evaluate which solutions are new and non‑obvious → potential IP (patent, design, or copyright).[[blog.ipleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/ipr-description/)]
    

So: real‑life problem → innovative solution → protect through IP → business/ startup opportunity.

## 7. Conducting analysis and registering claims for IP protection

Typical process (very often becomes exam question):

- Step 1: Identify the creation (invention / brand / creative work / design).
    
- Step 2: Prior art search – check existing patents, trademarks or works to see novelty and avoid conflict.[[kiteworks](https://www.kiteworks.com/risk-compliance-glossary/intellectual-property/)]
    
- Step 3: Decide appropriate IP type: patent, trademark, copyright, design, etc.[[wipo](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/about-ip)]
    
- Step 4: Prepare application (specifications, drawings, claims for patent; logo/word and class for trademark, etc.).[[wipo](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/about-ip)]
    
- Step 5: File with appropriate office (e.g., patent office, trademark registry, copyright office).[[wipo](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/about-ip)]
    
- Step 6: Examination, objections/clarifications, publication, grant/registration.[[wipo](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/about-ip)]
    
- Step 7: Maintain rights by paying renewal fees (for trademarks, patents).[[wipo](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/about-ip)]
    

For exam MCQs, focus on:

- Matching IP type ↔ what it protects.
    
- Meaning of different marks.
    
- Basic objectives and steps in using IP for business.

## Unit II – MCQ‑style Practice (30 Qs)

You can add options yourself when revising. Some are concept, some scenario‑based.

1. Intellectual Property primarily refers to:  
    – Creations of the mind such as inventions, literary and artistic works, designs, and symbols used in commerce.
    
2. The main purpose of IP laws is to:  
    – Encourage innovation/creativity by giving creators exclusive rights for a limited period.
    
3. Which of the following is NOT typically considered Intellectual Property?  
    – A piece of land used for a factory.
    
4. Copyright primarily protects:  
    – Original expressions such as books, music, films, paintings, or software code.
    
5. An exam theory question paper set by a teacher is mainly protected under:  
    – Copyright.
    
6. A student writes original Android app code for a project. Protection of this code as a literary work would come under:  
    – Copyright.
    
7. Trademark mainly protects:  
    – Words, logos, symbols, or designs that identify and distinguish goods or services of a business.
    
8. A unique symbol used by a software company to identify its product on websites and packaging is an example of:  
    – Logo used as a trademark.
    
9. A mark used for identifying IT consulting services (not products) would be called:  
    – Service mark.
    
10. A label used by an independent body to certify that certain products meet a particular quality standard is a:  
    – Certification mark.
    
11. A famous brand name that is widely recognized by the public and given extra legal protection is called a:  
    – Well‑known mark.
    
12. A mark used by members of a professional association (for example, all members of a specific industry association) is called a:  
    – Collective mark.
    
13. A catchy name and logo used together for a mobile wallet app would be best described as:  
    – Brand name and logo functioning as a trademark.
    
14. A patent is usually granted for:  
    – A new and useful invention that involves an inventive step and is industrially applicable.
    
15. Which of the following would most likely be patentable?  
    – A new process that reduces electricity consumption of servers by 30%.
    
16. Once a patent is granted, the patent holder can:  
    – Prevent others from making, using, or selling the invention without permission for the patent term.
    
17. When the term of a patent expires:  
    – The invention enters the public domain and can be used by anyone.
    
18. A startup studies published patents in cloud‑computing security before designing its product. What is the main benefit?  
    – Avoiding duplication and identifying gaps where they can innovate.
    
19. Patent information helps in business development mainly by:  
    – Revealing technology trends and competitor inventions, useful for planning R&D and strategy.
    
20. If a company develops a new logo but finds a similar mark already registered in the same class, the problem relates to:  
    – Trademark conflict discovered during prior search.
    
21. A small IT company wants to expand globally with its own brand name. Which IP should it register first to protect its brand identity?  
    – Trademark for its brand name and logo.
    
22. A team designs a new graphical user interface (GUI) that is visually unique but not technically different. Which form of IP is most relevant?  
    – Design/copyright depending on jurisdiction (visual appearance).
    
23. Which pair is correctly matched?  
    – Patent → protects technical inventions.
    
24. Which step will usually be done BEFORE filing a patent application?  
    – Conduct prior art search to check novelty.
    
25. In a social‑engineering project, students identify poor waste management in a city and design an IoT‑based smart bin system. To protect their technical solution they should mainly look at:  
    – Filing a patent for their IoT system design or method.
    
26. A startup’s logo is copied by another company providing similar IT services. Which right is primarily infringed?  
    – Trademark right.
    
27. Which of the following is an example of using IP to create business value?  
    – Licensing a patented technology to another company for royalty income.
    
28. While analyzing a local engineering issue, a team finds that farmers struggle with monitoring soil moisture. They design a low‑cost sensor solution. The process of converting this solution into IP mainly starts with:  
    – Documenting the invention and checking novelty through patent search.
    
29. “Expression protected, not abstract idea” is a principle most closely associated with:  
    – Copyright.
    
30. In the context of this course, identifying social issues and registering IP claims are encouraged mainly because:  
    – They link real‑life problem solving with innovation, protection, and startup opportunities.
